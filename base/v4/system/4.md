# Four

**Vowel pattern: `i o u e` — around the cross, up right down left.**

```text
      i           i --> o
   e  a  o        ^     |
      u           e <-- u
```

**Four is the four directions**, and that is not a metaphor. The path
goes up, right, down, left: it walks the four arms of the cross in
order and comes back where it started, never touching the centre.

Nothing else the cross can do has four steps. This is why four is the
size for north, east, south, west, and why a four in Tune is heard as
going around something.

Four is also **two twos**, the two axes crossed. Both readings are the
same path: around a square is the same as picking one end of each of
two axes, four times.

## The abstract four

> **two independent binaries**

```text
        + second
           |
  − first ─┼─ + first
           |
        − second
```

Every clean four is this. North/south crossed with east/west. Hot/cold
crossed with wet/dry. Past/future crossed with real/possible.

**A four should be named as two twos wherever it really is one.** Where
it is not, the four is probably conventional rather than fundamental.

## Four from two twos

Worth building deliberately, because each is really a crossing:

```text
temperature x moisture     hot-dry  hot-wet  cold-dry  cold-wet
time x reality             was  is  could-have  could-be
self x other               I-you  I-them  we-you  we-them
give x take                give  take  keep  refuse
```

## Where to put each member

The path is a rotation, so **opposites land opposite**. Members 1 and 3
are `i` and `u`, the top and bottom arms. Members 2 and 4 are `o` and
`e`, the right and left arms.

```text
1  i   up      north    +y
2  o   right   east     +x
3  u   down    south    −y
4  e   left    west     −x
```

So when a four really is two crossed axes, **put each axis on a
diagonal**: one axis at positions 1 and 3, the other at 2 and 4. Then a
member and its opposite are a vowel apart in the obvious way, and the
set can be said in reverse to go round the other way.

For north/east/south/west this is already true, and the vowels line up
with the compass exactly.
