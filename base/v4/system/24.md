# Twenty four

**No vowel pattern yet.**

This is one of two sizes with a file here and no path on the cross. It
wants one, and the shape of the set should decide which.

## What a twenty four could be

```text
24 = 12 x 2      the twelve, run twice
24 = 8 x 3       the star, three times
24 = 6 x 4       the six, four times
24 = 4 x 6       around the cross, six times
```

**The twelve doubled is the obvious candidate**, because that is what a
day is: two twelves, morning and afternoon. It would be the
[twelve](12.md) path run twice over, which is the three run eight times,
and by then the pattern has stopped saying anything.

**The rotation six times is the better bet.** A day is a cycle, and the
[sixteen](16.md) shows that cycles want the four-rotation rather than
the axis. `i o u e` six times gives twenty four with the hours falling
on the same arm every four hours.

```text
i o u e  i o u e  i o u e  i o u e  i o u e  i o u e
```

That is worth writing down and trying against real hour words.

## The abstract twenty four

> **the most divisible number of its size**

```text
24 / 2 = 12     24 / 3 = 8      24 / 4 = 6
24 / 6 = 4      24 / 8 = 3      24 / 12 = 2
```

Six proper divisors above one. Twenty four divides better than anything
smaller, which is the same argument [twelve](12.md) makes, one level up.

## Fundamental

| domain | the twenty four | why it is clean |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| hours | the day divided | one Earth rotation, split by twelves |
| divisibility | 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 | more divisors than any smaller number |
| the 24-cell | a regular 4-polytope with no 3D analogue | exists only in four dimensions |
| rotations of a cube | exactly twenty four | the rotation group of a cube |
| `4!` | twenty four orderings of four things | the first interesting factorial |

**The cube's rotation group is the strongest.** There are exactly
twenty four ways to turn a cube onto itself, and that is a countable
fact, not a convention. It also connects straight to the
[eight](8.md) corners and the [six](6.md) faces.

**The 24-cell is a genuine curiosity.** It is the one regular polytope
that exists in four dimensions and has no analogue in three or in five.
Nothing else in this file is that special.

## Why the day is twenty four

Not fundamental. The Egyptians used ten daytime hours plus two twilight,
and twelve night hours from twelve decan stars, and the two twelves
stuck. A day could as easily have been ten hours or twenty.

**Worth having because everybody lives in it**, on the same argument as
the [ten](10.md).

## Candidate words

Already in `potential.csv`: `hour` `day` `night` `morning` `evening`
`noon` `midnight` `turn` `cube` `gold`.

**Missing:** nothing wanted. The hours are named by number in every
language that has them, so a twenty four here is the
[sixteen](16.md) numbers doing their job, not twenty four new roots.

## Advice

**Do not spend twenty four base words on anything.** At this size a set
should be built by compounding a smaller system with a number, which is
what every natural language does with hours.

The one reason to give twenty four a vowel path is if some set really
does have twenty four equal members that are not numbered. The cube's
rotations are the only candidate in the table, and they are technical.

**So the likely answer is that twenty four needs no pattern**, and this
file exists to record why rather than to fill a gap.
