# Nine

**Vowel pattern: `i e a o u o a e i` — the snake out and the snake
back.**

```text
      i
   e  a  o        i e a o u   then   o a e i
      u
```

The [five](5.md) snake traced forward, then retraced from the bottom
back to the top. It is a palindrome and the turn happens **on** the
bottom arm, which is said once.

Nine is therefore **a five that comes home**. Members 1 and 9 are both
`i`, members 2 and 8 both `e`, and so on, with member 5 alone on `u`.

## What the fold says

The path pairs member 1 with 9, 2 with 8, 3 with 7, 4 with 6, and
leaves 5 by itself in the middle.

**So a nine is four pairs around a centre.** If the nine really is a
3×3 grid that is exactly right: the corners and edges pair off across
the middle square, and the middle square is member 5.

```text
1 2 3
4 5 6      read as a spiral or a boustrophedon, 5 is always the centre
7 8 9
```

## The abstract nine

> **a three of threes**

```text
3 x 3 = 9
```

Nine is the first square of an odd number, which is what gives it a true
centre. A [four](4.md) has no middle cell and an [eight](8.md) has no
middle member, but a nine does, and the vowel path puts it there.

Any two independent ternaries cross into a nine:

```text
past/present/future  x  near/mid/far
less/equal/greater   x  small/medium/large
-/0/+                x  -/0/+
```

That last one is worth noting: **the sign three crossed with itself** is
a nine, and covers every combination of two signed quantities.

## Fundamental

| domain | the nine | why it is clean |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| a 3×3 grid | nine cells with a true centre | the smallest square with a middle |
| two ternaries crossed | 3 × 3 | any pair of independent threes |
| nonzero decimal digits | 1 through 9 | what is left of ten without zero |
| a magic square, smallest | the 3×3, unique up to symmetry | there is exactly one |

**Nine is thin on fundamentals**, and that is honest. It is a square, it
has a centre, and most of what it does is cross two threes. Everything
else at this size is convention.

## Conventional

| domain | the nine |
| :--- | :--- |
| the planets, once | Mercury through Pluto |
| the Enneagram | nine types |
| innings | nine |
| the Muses | nine |
| cat's lives | nine |
| Beethoven's symphonies | nine |
| circles of Hell | nine |

**The nine planets are the cautionary case.** The count changed when the
definition changed, which is the mark of a convention rather than a
fact. A Tune system for the planets should be built on what is actually
there, and be ready to be wrong.

## Advice

**Prefer building a nine as a cross of two threes** rather than as nine
separate things. The vowel path supports it: the pairs fold around the
centre, so a nine laid out as `3 × 3` has its structure audible, while a
nine laid out as a flat list does not.

If the nine is not a cross of threes, check whether it is really a
[ten](10.md) with one member missing, or an [eight](8.md) with one
added.
