# Ten

**Vowel pattern: `i e a o u u o a e i` — the snake out and back,
turning after the end.**

```text
      i
   e  a  o        i e a o u | u o a e i
      u
```

The same fold as the [nine](9.md), except the turn happens **after** the
bottom arm rather than on it, so `u` is said twice and the pattern has
no centre, only a seam.

That is the whole difference between nine and ten: **a nine has a
middle, a ten has a join.** Members 5 and 6 are the two halves meeting.

## What the seam says

The path pairs 1 with 10, 2 with 9, 3 with 8, 4 with 7, 5 with 6. Every
member has a partner and none is left over.

**So a ten is five pairs**, and the natural layout is a thing and its
opposite, five times. That is also what two hands are.

## The abstract ten

There is no deep ten. Ten is `2 × 5`, and five is the number of fingers
on a hand, and that is the entire reason the world counts in tens.

```text
10 = 2 x 5      two hands, five fingers
```

It is not a power of two, it divides only by 2 and 5, and it is worse
at division than [twelve](12.md). **Ten is an accident of anatomy**, and
worth having only because everybody has it.

That is not nothing. A number system everybody already knows is worth
more than a better one nobody does.

## Fundamental

| domain | the ten | why it is clean |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| fingers | two hands of five | the reason for all the rest |
| decimal digits | 0 through 9 | what those fingers became |

**That is the whole list**, and the second entry is downstream of the
first. Ten has no other fundamental claim: no geometry, no binary
structure, no counting argument that does not go through hands.

Compare [twelve](12.md), which divides four ways, or [sixteen](16.md),
which is four bits. Ten beats neither and is used by everyone.

## Where ten is genuinely useful

**As the size of the digit set.** Tune already has fourteen of sixteen
numbers named in `../term/list.csv`, built for [sixteen](16.md) rather
than ten, because 4,096 is 16³ and three digits name any base word
exactly.

If Tune also wants decimal, the ten is the first ten of those sixteen,
not a separate set. Do not name ten digits and sixteen digits
separately.

## Advice

**Do not build a ten unless it is about hands or about decimal.** Almost
every other ten in the world is a round number somebody stopped at, and
the underlying set is a [nine](9.md), a [twelve](12.md) or a
[sixteen](16.md).

The vowel path helps here: if the thing does not fall into five natural
pairs, it is probably not a ten.
