# Five

**Vowel pattern: `i e a o u` — a snake through the whole cross.**

```text
      i           i
   e  a  o        e  a  o
      u                 u
```

Top, left, centre, right, bottom. **Five is the only size that touches
every point of the cross exactly once**, and the only way to do that is
to weave: down to the left arm, across through the centre, out to the
right arm, and down.

So a five is the complete set. Nothing is left over and nothing is
visited twice.

## The abstract five

> **a three with its ends split**

```text
three          −        0        +
five      −−   −        0        +   ++
```

A five is a three that has learned to say *how much*. Where a three
gives direction, a five gives direction and degree, and that covers
almost every scalar property there is.

```text
very low   low    middle   high    very high
tiny       small  medium   large   huge
freezing   cold   mild     hot     burning
hate       dislike neutral like    love
never      rarely sometimes often  always
none       few    some     many    all
```

**This may be worth more to Tune than any culturally standard five**,
because it generalises to every graded quality rather than naming one
list. It is also why the pattern walks the whole cross: a five is not a
set of five things, it is a full range.

## Fundamental

| domain | the five | why it is clean |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| magnitude | `−−  −  0  +  ++` | the graded three, the abstract five |
| taste | sweet · sour · salty · bitter · umami | the five receptor types |
| Platonic solids | tetrahedron · cube · octahedron · dodecahedron · icosahedron | exactly five exist, provably |
| the cross itself | centre · up · down · left · right | a point and its four directions |
| digits per hand | thumb · index · middle · ring · little | why ten and why five |
| regular tilings by symmetry | fivefold rotation | the one that cannot tile a plane |

**The Platonic solids are the strongest five in the table**, because
five is not a convention there. Euclid proved there are exactly five and
there cannot be a sixth.

## Senses

The traditional five are **sight · hearing · smell · taste · touch**,
and they are worth having as a set. But modern sensory physiology counts
more than five: balance, body position, temperature, pain and the
internal senses are all distinct systems with their own receptors.

So this is a **conventional** five wearing fundamental clothes. Worth a
set, worth knowing it is ours.

## Element systems

Three cultures reached five independently, which is interesting in
itself:

| tradition | the five |
| :--- | :--- |
| Chinese Wuxing | wood · fire · earth · metal · water |
| Indian Mahābhūta | earth · water · fire · air · ether |
| Japanese Godai | earth · water · fire · wind · void |

None is a decomposition of matter. All three are attempts at exactly
the thing Tune is doing: **a small conceptual coordinate system for
reality.** Worth studying as prior art rather than adopting.

## The liquid problem

The path uses every vowel, so a five always includes members on `i`,
`e` and `u`. **A liquid closes only on `a` or `o`**, so three of any
five cannot close on `l` or `r`.

That bites here harder than anywhere except the nine. Choose consonants
for a five knowing that only members 3 and 4 can end in a liquid.
