# Eight

**Vowel pattern: `a i a o a u a e` — a star, out to each point and home
between.**

```text
      i
   e  a  o        a-i  a-o  a-u  a-e
      u
```

Centre, up, centre, right, centre, down, centre, left. **Every other
word carries `a`**, so an eight is heard as four spokes rather than
eight things, and the centre is touched four times.

This is the one pattern that is not a path around or across. It is a
path that keeps returning.

## What the star says about an eight

An eight is **four things and their opposites**, and the vowel says so.
Members 2, 4, 6, 8 take the four directions; members 1, 3, 5, 7 take
the centre.

So the natural reading is four pairs, each pair being a departure from
the middle and a return to it. Lay an eight out that way and the pattern
carries the structure. Lay it out as eight unrelated things and the
pattern is saying something the content does not.

## The abstract eight

> **three binary splits**

```text
2 x 2 x 2 = 8
```

Three independent yes-or-no questions divide anything into eight. That
is the deep eight, and it is why eight keeps arriving wherever space and
binary structure meet.

```text
±x  ±y  ±z      the eight octants of space
000 ... 111     the eight three-bit numbers
```

## Fundamental

| domain | the eight | why it is clean |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| octants | the eight corners of `±x ±y ±z` | three axes, each split |
| three bits | `000` to `111` | 2³, the same eight |
| a cube | eight vertices | the same eight again, made solid |
| stable nucleon shells | 2 · 8 · 20 · 28 · 50 · 82 · 126 | eight is a magic number |
| the octet rule | eight valence electrons | why the periodic table has the shape it does |
| gluons | eight of them | a fact about the strong force |
| the queen's moves | eight directions on a grid | the same eight as the compass |

**The octants and the three bits are the same eight.** So is the cube's
corner count and the compass. Anything that splits three ways in a
binary fashion lands here, and that is most of what eight is good for.

## The compass eight

```text
N  NE  E  SE  S  SW  W  NW
```

This is a [four](4.md) with its diagonals filled in, which makes it the
one eight that is **not** three binary splits. It is a rotation with
twice the resolution.

Worth noting because it fights the star pattern: a compass eight has no
centre and no pairing into spokes, it goes around. If Tune wants the
eight-point compass, the sixteen path suits it better than the eight
path does, and the eight path suits `±x ±y ±z` better.

## Conventional

| domain | the eight |
| :--- | :--- |
| a byte | eight bits, by convention that stuck |
| the trigrams | the eight of the I Ching |
| an octave | eight notes counting both ends |
| an octopus | eight arms |
| a spider | eight legs |
| the noble eightfold path | eight |
| a stop sign | eight sides |

**The octave is a counting artefact.** There are seven notes and the
eighth is the first again. Worth knowing before building an eight on it.

## Candidate words

Already in `potential.csv`: `corner` `bit` `byte` `north` `south`
`east` `west` `eight` `cube` `shell` `arm` `leg`.

**Missing:** the intercardinal names, which English builds by
compounding rather than with roots. Tune should probably do the same
rather than spend eight base words on a compass.

## Which eight to build first

**The octants**, if any. They are the only eight that carries
structure, they match the star pattern, and they connect directly to
the [three](3.md) axes and the [six](6.md) directions.

The compass eight and the byte are better handled by compounding than
by eight base words.
