# Two

**Vowel pattern: `e o` — the flat axis, left then right.**

```text
      i
   e  a  o        e --> o
      u
```

The two uses the horizontal arms of the cross and leaves the vertical
pair for the three. A two is an axis with the middle taken out: the two
ends of one line, and nothing between them.

Two is the smallest distinction there is. Everything larger is built by
cutting again.

## The abstract two

Almost every fundamental pair is one structure:

> **one side, and the other**

The question a two always answers is *which side*, and the sides are
always exchangeable by some flip. That makes two the only size where
the set carries no order of its own: `on off` and `off on` are the same
set, and only convention says which is first.

Tune should have **one word pair for polarity itself**, and let the
specific pairs name their domain rather than re-encode the opposition.

## Fundamental

**A domain belongs to one size only.** Anything with a middle is a
three and lives in [3.md](3.md), however tempting it is to name the two
ends and stop. Charge is not `negative · positive`, it is
`negative · neutral · positive`. Sign, time, causation, comparison and
topology all have middles, and all of them are threes.

What is left here is the set of domains where **there is genuinely
nothing between**.

| domain | the two | why there is no middle |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| existence | is · is not | the deepest one |
| logic | true · false | classical truth admits no third |
| information | 0 · 1 | one bit, the unit of information |
| parity | even · odd | every integer is exactly one |
| spin | up · down | measured on an axis, one or the other |
| magnetism | north · south | a pole is never alone and never neutral |
| change | same · different | a thing is the same or it is not |
| life | alive · dead | for a thing that can be either |
| sex | female · male | two gamete sizes, which is what the word names |
| sound | silence · sound | none, or some |
| light | dark · light | none, or some |
| motion | still · moving | none, or some |
| opening | shut · open | no way through, or some way through |
| identity | self · other | other is simply not self |
| causation | cause · effect | the process between is the arrow, not a third thing |
| flow | input · output | likewise, until the middle is named |

## The third way to get a two: a directed pair

`cause · effect` is not a `none · some` and not a negation, and it still
has no middle. It is **two things and an arrow**.

Naming the arrow does not make a third member. "Process" is what the
relation IS, not something standing between the two like a fencepost.
Sender and receiver are two, and the sending is the arrow. Giver and
receiver are two, and the giving is the arrow.

**The test: can you point at the middle separately from the ends?**

```text
source  --path--  goal      the path is terrain you cross      a three
cause   --->      effect    the arrow is not a place           a two
```

`source · path · goal` is a genuine three because the path is a thing in
its own right: you can be on it, measure it, block it.
`beginning · middle · end` likewise, because the middle is part of the
duration. `subject · verb · object` likewise, because the verb is a real
element of the sentence.

Causation has none of that. Take the arrow away and nothing is left over
to name.

## The other way to get a two: negation

Beside `none · some` there is a second shape, and it is just as clean.

**Cut everything in half with a `not`.** `self` and `other` is one:
other is not-self, and there is nothing between being oneself and not
being oneself. The world is not a middle term, it is both halves added
back together.

`same · different` works the same way, and so does `is · is not`. A
partition by negation is always exactly two, because a thing either
satisfies the predicate or it does not.

```text
bounded at zero      none ......... some
negation             X ............ not X
```

Both give twos. Anything that is neither is probably a three.

## The test: can it go negative?

Sharper than asking what sits in the middle, and it decides every case.

**If the quantity is bounded at zero, the domain is a two.** There is no
negative sound. There is no negative light, no negative speed, no
negative openness. Zero is an end, not a middle, so the set is
`none · some` and nothing lies between.

Quiet is not halfway between silence and sound. **Quiet is a small
amount of sound**, sitting on the sound side. Ajar is a partly open
door, sitting on the open side. Slow is a small speed, sitting on the
moving side.

**If the quantity runs both ways from a middle, the domain is a three.**
Temperature does: hot and cold are both real departures from neutral,
and neither is the absence of the other. So are charge, velocity,
elevation and value.

```text
bounded at zero      none ......... some              a two
runs both ways       cold ... neutral ... hot         a three
```

Temperature is the clean counter-example and worth holding onto. It
looks exactly like sound and is the opposite case.

## Looks like a two, is a three

The traps. Each is worth a word, and the word belongs in [3.md](3.md).

| looks like | the middle it hides | goes negative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| negative · positive | zero | yes, by definition |
| hot · cold | warm | yes, either side of neutral |
| before · after | at the same time | yes, time runs both ways from now |
| less · greater | equal | yes |
| up · down | level | yes, height either side of the ground |
| inside · outside | the boundary | yes, depth either side of the surface |
| cause · effect | the process between | yes, the arrow runs both ways |
| left · right | centred, aligned, achiral | yes |
| decrease · increase | unchanged | yes |

Every one has **two real ends and a middle**. None is a case of having
none of something, which is why none of them belongs here.

## The deepest pair

**`is` and `is not`.** Every other two is a special case of something
either being there or not being there. If only one pair got a Tune word,
it should be this one.

## Conventional

Real, useful, and could have come out otherwise.

Several of these have middles too. They are here because the pair is
the useful thing even so, not because the middle is missing.

## Candidate words

Already in `potential.csv`: `true` `false` `same` `different` `alive`
`dead` `female` `male` `even` `odd` `sound` `silence` `light` `dark`
`still` `open` `shut`.

**Missing:** a word for polarity itself, which English has no clean
single word for. `pole` is the nearest and is in the list. Also `spin`
and `parity` as such, though those are technical enough not to need a
base word.

The pairs listed under the traps above are candidates for
[3.md](3.md), not for here, so their words are counted there.

## Which side is `e`

Because a two has no natural first, **the vowel supplies one**, and the
order becomes part of the system rather than the speaker's choice.

The convention worth keeping: **`e` is the absence, `o` is the
presence.** `e` for false, off, still, silent, dead, out, zero, not.
`o` for true, on, moving, sounding, alive, in, one, is.

That way the vowel alone carries polarity across every pair in the
language, and a listener who has never met a particular pair still
knows which half they were handed.

It also sits right on the cross. `e` is the left arm and `o` is the
right, and left to right is the direction a two is read in, so the
absent side is the one you start from and the present side is the one
you arrive at.
