# Three

**Vowel pattern: `i a u` — the upright axis, top through centre to
bottom.**

```text
      i           i
   e  a  o        a
      u           u
```

The three is the first path that **passes through the middle**. A two is
the two ends of a line. A three is the two ends and the point between
them, which is the whole of what a three is for.

Three is the first size with a **middle**, and that is the whole of what
three is for. A two has sides. A three has sides and a place between
them.

**Most things that look like twos are threes.** If something sits
exactly between the two ends and is worth naming, the domain belongs
here. [2.md](2.md) keeps a list of the traps, and it is longer than its
list of real twos.

## The three abstract threes

Most fundamental triples are one of these, and naming the structure once
is worth more than naming every instance.

### 1. `− 0 +` · signed relation

A centre, and a departure from it either way.

```text
negative   zero        positive
less       equal       greater
decrease   remain      increase
before     during      after
below      level       above
behind     at          ahead
against    indifferent for
bad        neutral     good
```

**This may deserve one Tune triple of its own**, rather than encoding
every row separately. It is the single most productive ternary there is.

### 2. `origin → middle → destination` · directed process

```text
past       present     future
beginning  middle      end
input      process     output
source     path        goal
```

Anything that goes from somewhere to somewhere has this shape, **so long
as the middle is a thing you can point at.**

`cause · effect` is not on this list. Its middle is the arrow itself,
and naming the arrow does not make a third member, so causation is a two
and lives in [2.md](2.md).

`input · process · output` stays, because the process is a real stage:
the machine, the computation, the step that runs. You can point at it,
time it, replace it. Take the middle out of `input · output` and it is a
two like causation. Put a nameable transformation back in and it is a
three again.

**The same pair can be either**, and which one it is depends on whether
the middle is being named.

`sender · message · receiver` and `birth · life · death` are off the
list for a different reason, below.

## Members must be the same kind of thing

A system is a set its members can be compared within. If one member is
not the same sort of thing as the others, **it is not a member, it is
scenery**.

```text
sender   message   receiver     two people and an object
birth    life      death        two events and a duration
```

Neither is a three. `sender · receiver` is two people and a two.
`birth · death` is two events and a two. The message and the life are
real and important, and they belong to a different set.

The test is whether the members are **interchangeable in a sentence**.
You can ask which of past, present and future something is in. You
cannot ask whether something is a sender, a message or a receiver and
expect the question to mean one thing.

This is the criterion that cuts hardest, and several entries below
survive it only on a reading:

| set | survives if |
| :--- | :--- |
| `source · path · goal` | read as three ROLES in a motion, not a place, a route and a place |
| `input · process · output` | read as three STAGES, not two data and an operation |
| `subject · verb · object` | read as three SLOTS in a clause, which is how grammar means it |

**Decide the reading once per set.** Under the role reading they are
clean threes and belong here. Under the object reading they are twos
with scenery, and the middles want their own words elsewhere.

### 3. `one · relation · other` · mediated pair

```text
subject    verb        object
part       joint       part
speaker    speech      listener
giver      gift        receiver
```

The three that makes a two into a sentence. Note this is **not**
`self · other`, which has no middle and is a two. What makes these
threes is that the thing in the middle is real: the verb, the joint, the
speech, the gift.

## The ten strongest

If only ten triples got words:

```text
SPACE        x · y · z
SIGN         − · 0 · +
ORDER        less · equal · greater
TIME         past · present · future
CHANGE       decrease · remain · increase
BOUNDARY     inside · boundary · outside
PATH         source · path · goal
QUANTITY     none · one · many
PERSON       speaker · listener · other
VALUE        bad · neutral · good
```

**`self · other` is not among them**, though it looks like it should be.
Other is simply not-self, so that is a two and lives in [2.md](2.md).
What makes PERSON a three is that the listener is a real third thing and
not the negation of anything: speaker, the one spoken to, and the one
spoken about.

These are not lists of three things. They are **structures** that model
large parts of experience, and most of the table above reduces to them.

## The `a` in the middle

The pattern puts `a` on the middle member of every three, and `a` is the
centre of the cross. So **the neutral, the zero, the present, the
boundary and the path all carry `a`** across every triple in the
language. The extremes take `i` and `u` and the centre takes `a`, so the
shape of a ternary is audible before its content is known.
