# Six

**Vowel pattern: `i a u e a o` — both axes, upright then flat.**

```text
      i           i            .
   e  a  o        a    then    e  a  o
      u           u            .
```

The upright axis through the centre, then the flat axis through the
centre. **Six is three twice**, and on the cross it is plainly two
threes rather than three twos.

`a` lands on members 2 and 5, so a six is heard as two triples, each
passing through the middle.

## The abstract six

> **three axes, each taken both ways**

```text
3 axes x 2 directions = 6
```

This is the six that matters. Three is the number of spatial axes, and
six is what you get when you stop treating an axis as one thing and
start treating each end as its own direction.

```text
        +y  up
         |
 -x ---- o ---- +x      left, right
         |
        -y  down

        plus +z front, -z back
```

Six is therefore **the directions**, where three is **the dimensions**.
That is the cleanest division of labour between the two sizes, and it
is worth keeping strictly: never put "up, down, left, right, front,
back" in the three, and never put "x, y, z" in the six.

## Fundamental

| domain | the six | why it is clean |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| direction | up · down · left · right · front · back | three axes, both ways |
| cube | six faces | the same six, made solid |
| quark flavours | up · down · charm · strange · top · bottom | six, in three generations of two |
| lepton flavours | electron · muon · tau and their neutrinos | six likewise |
| degrees of freedom | three translations · three rotations | how a rigid body can move |
| a hexagon | the only regular polygon that tiles with its own edge length | why honeycombs |
| closest packing | six neighbours in a plane | how circles pack |

**Six faces and six directions are the same six.** A cube is what a
three-axis space looks like from inside, and that is why dice, rooms
and crystals keep arriving at six.

## Six as two threes

Because the vowel path is two triples, a six built as `3 × 2` should be
laid out so **the two triples are the two halves**.

```text
1  i   first axis, one way
2  a   first axis, centre
3  u   first axis, other way
4  e   second axis, one way
5  a   second axis, centre
6  o   second axis, other way
```

That works when the six really is two threes. When the six is three
twos, as the directions are, the pattern is fighting the content a
little: the natural pairing is 1-2, 3-4, 5-6 but the vowels group
1-2-3 and 4-5-6.

**Worth deciding once.** Either the directions are ordered
`up down left right front back`, which suits three twos, or
`up centre down · left centre right`, which suits the vowels and wants
a centre member, making it an eight.

Already in `potential.csv`: `up` `down` `left` `right` `front` `back`
`face` `cube` `strange` `top` `bottom` `turn`.

**Missing:** nothing important. The six directions are the strongest
set here and all six words exist.

## Which six to build first

**The directions.** They are the only six in the table that Tune will
use constantly, they are fully covered by `potential.csv`, and they
carry the geometry the whole system is built on.

Everything else at this size is either a physics set that needs no base
word or a convention.
