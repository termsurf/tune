# Systems

A **system** is a closed set of things the language names together and
counts on: the planets, the colours, the directions, the days. The set
has a known size and a known order, and every member of it is a word.

**The words of a system cycle their vowel to a set pattern.** The
consonants carry what each thing IS. The vowel carries where it sits in
the set, so a listener who knows the system hears the position without
being told it.

## The vowels are a cross

This is the whole key, and nothing below makes sense without it.

```text
      i
   e  a  o
      u
```

`a` is the centre. `i` is up and `u` is down. `e` is left and `o` is
right. The five vowels are not a line, they are **a point and four
directions from it**.

So a vowel pattern is not an order on a list. **It is a path traced on
the cross**, and the shape of that path is what the size means.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| `i u` | the up and down axis |
| `e o` | the left and right axis |
| `a` | the centre, on both |

## The patterns

| size | vowels | the path |
| ---: | :--- | :--- |
| 2 | `e o` | the flat axis, left then right |
| 3 | `i a u` | the upright axis, top through centre to bottom |
| 4 | `i o u e` | **around**, up right down left |
| 5 | `i e a o u` | a snake, top left centre right bottom |
| 6 | `i a u e a o` | both axes, upright then flat |
| 7 | `i o u e u o i` | around to the left, then back the way it came |
| 8 | `a i a o a u a e` | **a star**, out to each point and home between |
| 9 | `i e a o u o a e i` | the snake out and the snake back |
| 10 | `i e a o u u o a e i` | the snake out and back, turning after the end |
| 12 | `i a u u a i i a u u a i` | the upright axis, down and up, twice |
| 16 | `i o u e e o u i i o u e e o u i` | around, and around again, twice |

## What each path is

**Two is the flat axis.** `e o` is left then right. It uses the
horizontal pair and leaves the vertical pair for the three, which is why
a two is not `i u`.

**Three is the upright axis.** `i a u` goes top, centre, bottom. It is
the first path that passes through the middle, which is what a three is
for: two sides and the place between them.

**Four is the four directions.** `i o u e` is up, right, down, left. A
four goes **around the cross**, and that is why four is the size for
north, east, south, west. Nothing else the cross can do has four steps.

**Five is a snake.** `i e a o u` is top, left, centre, right, bottom. It
touches every point once, and the only way to do that is to weave: down
to the left arm, across through the centre, out to the right arm, and
down. Five is the size that uses the whole cross exactly once.

**Six is both axes.** `i a u` then `e a o` is the upright axis then the
flat one, each traced through the centre. Six is three twice, or two
threes, and on the cross it is plainly the latter.

**Seven is out and back.** `i o u e` around to the left arm, then
`u o i` retracing. It is a palindrome, and the turn happens **on** the
left arm, so that point is said once.

**Eight is a star.** `a i a o a u a e` returns to the centre between
every direction. Out to the top and home, out to the right and home, out
to the bottom and home, out to the left. Every other word carries `a`,
so an eight reads as four spokes rather than eight points, which is
what an eight usually is.

**Nine, ten and twelve repeat shorter paths.** Nine is the snake out and
back turning on the bottom. Ten is the same turning after it, so the
bottom is said twice. Twelve is the upright axis run down and up, twice
over.

## The sixteen does not close

Every other path is a shape you could draw without lifting the pen. The
sixteen is `i o u e` then `e o u i`, and the second half is not a
rotation: going around from the left arm would be `e u o i`, which is
left, bottom, right, top, the way round backwards.

What is written instead exchanges the top and the left and leaves the
other two, which is not a motion on the cross at all.

**So the sixteen is the one path that does not trace.** If it was meant
to go around and back, the way the seven does, it would be

```text
i o u e e u o i        around, then around the other way
```

doubled. That is a true palindrome and one vowel differs in each half
from what is written. Left as it stands rather than tidied, because it
may be deliberate.

## Which sizes have no pattern

**1, 11, 13, 14 and 15 are not set.** A one needs no vowel to tell its
members apart, having none to tell apart. Eleven and thirteen are prime,
so neither can be a doubling, and none of the folds land on them.

**24 and 36 are not set either**, and both have a file here, so both
want one. A twenty four is the twelve doubled or the eight tripled. A
thirty six is the twelve tripled or the six squared.

These are open, not decided against.

## How this meets the rest of v4

A system's words are ordinary v4 words and every rule still holds. In
particular **a liquid closes only on `a` or `o`**, so a member whose
path gives it `i`, `e` or `u` cannot close on `l` or `r`. That bites
most on the five and the nine, which walk the whole cross.

4,096 is 16 × 16 × 16, so a set of sixteen is a natural size here, and
the sixteen is the longest path written down for that reason.

## Reading a system aloud

Because the vowel is tracing a path, a system read in order sounds like
a movement rather than a list, and a member said out of order is heard
as out of place. That is the whole of what the patterns buy, and it is
why a set gets one path rather than each word choosing a vowel for its
own reasons.
