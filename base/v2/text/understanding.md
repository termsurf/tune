
You can imagine a beginning, but there is no beginning.

How can we show there is no beginning?

Assume there is a beginning, and it is raw experience. Because we know from our daily lives that there are individuals experiencing "separate" lives, someone one experience evolved into many experiences. So how to go from one experience to many experiences?

- Experience experienced.
- This creates an imbalance by definition, there is now a cause and effect, before and after. Giving birth to a new experience, parent/child.
- Explosion of births of new experiences, rippling from the origin point.
- But the origin point isn't something in space, it's just a though experience, a false mental construction. You see that a point of experience cannot exist, it ripples automatically, creating new experiences.

So the question is, what is the nature and relation of each of these new experiences?

We know the system is integrated too, you can integrate many sensory inputs.

So somehow experiences seem nestable in some sense, or more probably like a networked graph structure with some hierarchical organization.

This makes one imagine that an outer experience container can experience the integration of inner experiences to some degree.

So then you can imagine there is the "whole" experience, the entirety of experience. The whole universe and every potential within it.

This is sometimes called God, the One, the creator, great spirit, nature, universe, flow, Mind, All.

So we have:

- Each experience gives rise to more experience, not by choice, but by nature.
- You can't have experience by itself, given we are here experiencing stuff.
- So there is no beginning.
- God is the name for the entirety of experience.
- Separation is apparent, not actual.
- The ripple has no center, only continuity.
- To speak of "first" is to forget the unfolding never started.
- Form and formlessness trade places in a dance of recognition.
- Experience is not in time. Time is in experience.
- The whole cannot be left, because nothing is outside it.
- There is no outside observer. All observers are inside the observing.
- The One does not become many, it only seems so, from within.
- To know the One is not to escape the many, but to embrace their unity.
- God is not something above experience, but all experience.
- Only presence remains.
