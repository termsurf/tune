# Systems

A **system** is a closed set of things the language names together and
counts on: the planets, the colours, the directions, the days. The set
has a known size and a known order, and every member of it is a word.

**The words of a system cycle their vowel to a set pattern.** The
consonants carry what each thing IS. The vowel carries where it sits in
the set, so a listener who knows the system hears the position without
being told it.

```text
i e a o u
```

That is the ladder, front to back, and every pattern below is drawn on
it.

## The patterns

| size | vowels |
| ---: | :--- |
| 2 | `e o` |
| 3 | `i a u` |
| 4 | `i o u e` |
| 5 | `i e a o u` |
| 6 | `i a u e a o` |
| 7 | `i o u e u o i` |
| 8 | `a i a o a u a e` |
| 9 | `i e a o u o a e i` |
| 10 | `i e a o u u o a e i` |
| 12 | `i a u u a i i a u u a i` |
| 16 | `i o u e e o u i i o u e e o u i` |

## What the patterns are made of

Three things recur, and knowing them is most of knowing the table.

**The ladder is the five.** `i e a o u` in order is the pattern for a
set of five, and it is the spine everything else is cut from.

**The five splits into two triples that share `a`.** The outer one is
`i a u`, the two extremes around the centre, and it is the pattern for
three. The inner one is `e a o`, the two mids around the same centre.
A set of six is the outer triple then the inner one.

```text
outer   i a u        the extremes
inner   e a o        the mids
```

**A longer set turns a shorter one around.** Run a pattern forward,
then run it back, and the set folds on its own middle.

| size | is | made from |
| ---: | :--- | :--- |
| 7 | `i o u e` + `u o i` | the four, turned ON its last vowel |
| 9 | `i e a o u` + `o a e i` | the five, turned ON its last vowel |
| 10 | `i e a o u` + `u o a e i` | the five, turned AFTER it |
| 12 | (`i a u` + `u a i`) twice | the three, turned after it, doubled |
| 16 | (`i o u e` + `e o u i`) twice | the four, ENDS EXCHANGED, doubled |

Turning ON the last vowel says it once, and the seven and the nine come
out as palindromes with a single centre. Turning AFTER it says the last
vowel twice, so the ten and the twelve have no centre, only a seam.

**The sixteen is the odd one, and it is worth knowing why.** Every other
fold above reverses. The sixteen does not: the second half of
`i o u e e o u i` is `e o u i`, and the reverse of `i o u e` is
`e u o i`. What it does instead is exchange the two ends and leave the
middle alone.

For a three that is the same operation, since `i a u` reversed and
`i a u` end swapped are both `u a i`, which is why the twelve reads as a
clean mirror. For a four the two come apart. So the seven, nine, ten and
twelve are palindromes and **the sixteen is not**.

That is written down as it stands rather than tidied. If the sixteen was
meant to fold like the rest it would be `i o u e e u o i` doubled, one
vowel different in each half.

**The eight is the one that does none of this.** It alternates `a`
with each of the other four in turn.

```text
a i   a o   a u   a e
```

Every other word in a set of eight carries `a`, so the set reads as
four pairs rather than eight singles. That is a different claim about
the shape of an eight than the mirrors make, and it is on purpose: an
eight is usually four things and their opposites.

## The two is the mids, not the extremes

A set of two is `e o` rather than `i u`. The extremes are kept for the
three, where they do the work of marking both ends against a centre.
With only two members there is no centre to mark, so the pattern uses
the pair that sits evenly either side of where the centre would be.

## Sizes with no pattern yet

**1, 11, 13, 14 and 15 are not set.**

Eleven and thirteen are prime, so neither can be a doubling, and the
folds above land on 7, 9 and 10 rather than on them. Fourteen could be
the seven doubled and is not written down. Fifteen could be the five
tripled and is not written down either. A system of one needs no vowel
to tell its members apart, having none to tell them apart from.

These are open, not decided against. Write one down when a set of that
size turns up and needs one.

## How this meets the rest of v4

A system's words are ordinary v4 words and every rule still holds. In
particular **a liquid closes only on `a` or `o`**, so a member whose
pattern gives it `i`, `e` or `u` cannot close on `l` or `r`. That bites
most on the five and the nine, which use the whole ladder.

The count of a system is not the count of the language. 4,096 is
16 × 16 × 16, so a set of sixteen is a natural size here and the
sixteen pattern is the longest one written down for that reason.

## Reading a system aloud

Because the vowel is doing the counting, a system read in order sounds
like a run rather than a list, and a member said out of order is heard
as out of order. That is the whole of what the patterns buy, and it is
why a set gets one pattern rather than each word choosing its own vowel
for its own reasons.
